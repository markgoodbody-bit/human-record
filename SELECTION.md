# The Human Record — record selection

Status: **WORKING SELECTION ORIENTATION / NOT CANON / NOT A QUEUE / NOT A CLAIM OF GLOBAL PRIORITY**

The Human Record should not grow by collecting whatever is famous, easy to fetch, or emotionally compelling.

A new entry should earn its place because there is a specific recoverability or provenance problem that a record can actually help with.

```text
IMPORTANT != THR GAP
FAMOUS != AT RISK
AVAILABLE != WORTH RECORDING
SELECTION != SIGNIFICANCE
```

## The three losses to keep separate

### 1. Content loss

The thing itself may disappear or become unreadable:

- a degrading physical carrier;
- a vanishing website;
- obsolete software or hardware;
- a private or small collection with weak preservation;
- a recording or document with no durable copy.

THR may help with provenance, indexing, checksums, dependencies or routes to stronger preservation owners. It should not pretend that a metadata record is the preserved object.

`RECORD_OF_OBJECT != OBJECT_PRESERVED`

### 2. Lineage loss

The object or claim survives, but its origin and evidentiary ancestry become hard or impossible to reconstruct:

- repeated claims whose shared source disappears;
- creative work detached from creator/process evidence;
- copied media whose first public context is lost;
- derivative versions that look like independent witnesses;
- synthetic recombination that obscures what came from where.

This is the Human Record's strongest natural territory.

### 3. Living-knowledge loss

A recording or description survives while the living system that knows how to use, interpret, perform or transmit the knowledge disappears.

Examples can include:

- endangered craft practice;
- oral tradition;
- minority or endangered language knowledge;
- place-based ecological knowledge;
- performance practice;
- embodied repair or making skill.

A record can assist, but cannot substitute for living transmission.

```text
RECORDING != CONTINUATION
DOCUMENTATION != LIVING PRACTICE
PRESERVATION != EXTRACTION
```

## Selection signals

A candidate becomes more interesting when several of these are true:

- the current carrier, practitioner, creator, community or source is singular or ageing;
- knowledge is tacit, oral, embodied or context-dependent;
- institutional custody is weak or fragmented;
- access depends on an obsolete format, platform, service, operating environment or piece of hardware;
- source ancestry is already blurring through copying, reposting or recombination;
- synthetic abundance is likely to make human provenance materially harder to recover;
- a correction or provenance trail can still be captured now but is likely to harden into UNKNOWN later;
- the evidence needed to make a useful record is public or can be recorded with legitimate permission;
- a small record would preserve something a stronger owner is not already preserving adequately.

No one signal is a score or automatic inclusion rule.

## Strongest-owner check

Before opening a new record, ask who already owns the problem better.

Examples may include:

- archives and libraries for preservation;
- museums and collection owners for object records;
- language/community institutions for living language work;
- specialist craft bodies for craft viability;
- C2PA / Content Credentials and related standards for embedded digital provenance;
- scholarly repositories for research data;
- software-preservation organisations for executable environments and emulation.

If a stronger owner already preserves the material and its lineage adequately, THR should route outward rather than duplicate it.

```text
OWNER_FOUND + NO CONSEQUENTIAL GAP -> ROUTE / STOP
THR != UNIVERSAL ARCHIVE
```

A THR record can still be useful when it documents a specific relationship between sources, corrections or provenance that falls between those owners.

## Living people and communities

Risk does not create permission.

For living creators, practitioners, families or communities, selection must consider:

- who has legitimate authority to share the material;
- whether public recording is wanted;
- privacy, safety and cultural restrictions;
- whether some knowledge should remain restricted, local, sacred or unpublished;
- whether the record would transfer control away from the people whose knowledge it is;
- whether attribution and benefit are being preserved rather than extracted.

If useful preservation requires overriding legitimate holders, stop or redesign the record.

```text
RECORDING != OWNING
ACCESS != CONSENT
PUBLIC INTEREST != AUTOMATIC PUBLICATION AUTHORITY
COMMUNITY_KNOWLEDGE != PUBLIC_DOMAIN
```

THR may need records whose public layer says that some material exists and is deliberately not reproduced.

## Candidate quarry — not a queue

The 16 September 2026 risk scan identified these families as useful places to look for bounded future cases:

- endangered craft / embodied technical skill;
- vulnerable audiovisual or oral records;
- contemporary human digital creation with inspectable creator provenance;
- web-native or community material at real risk of disappearing;
- born-digital/software-dependent creative work;
- endangered language or community knowledge only where recording is community-led and appropriate.

These are discovery directions, not assignments and not a ranking of human value.

## Selection record

Every new Human Record entry should preserve its own selection provenance where practical:

- selection date;
- who or what chose it;
- why this object/case was chosen;
- what risk or provenance problem it tests;
- the candidate pool or alternatives considered, if known;
- if the pool/alternatives were not preserved, say that explicitly;
- what the selection does **not** imply about importance, representativeness, merit or canon;
- what would cause the proposed record to be stopped or routed elsewhere.

The first Homer specimen taught this by failure: its candidate pool and alternatives were not recorded. Future records should not silently repeat that gap.

## Bounded candidate test

Before spending heavily on a new record:

```text
REAL AT-RISK OBJECT / PRACTICE / CLAIM?
-> STRONGEST OWNER?
-> SPECIFIC LINEAGE / RECOVERABILITY GAP?
-> LEGITIMATE ACCESS / CONSENT?
-> SMALLEST USEFUL RECORD?
-> CAN UNKNOWN REMAIN VISIBLE?
-> CAN CORRECTION REMAIN OPEN?
-> BUILD OR ROUTE / STOP
```

A good result can be:

```text
OWNER FOUND
NO DELTA
NO CONSENT
NOT PUBLIC
NOT OUR GAP
STOP
```

## Relationship to the record contract

`SELECTION.md` answers **why this record?**

`RECORD_CONTRACT.md` answers **what must the record make inspectable once selected?**

Neither document decides historical truth, aesthetic merit, cultural importance or governance authority.
