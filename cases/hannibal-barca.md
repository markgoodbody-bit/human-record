# Human Record — historical human entity case: Hannibal Barca

Status: **WORKING RECORD CANDIDATE / PUBLIC SOURCES ONLY / HISTORICAL HUMAN / SOURCE-ANCESTRY AND IDENTITY STRESS CASE / NOT A BIOGRAPHY / NOT YET IN PUBLIC CATALOGUE / OPEN TO CORRECTION**

This record asks a narrow question:

> By what surviving evidence paths is the historical human commonly identified as Hannibal Barca reachable to us now, and how should The Human Record keep the entity separate from names, claims, later representations and missing source traditions?

It does **not** attempt to write a definitive biography of Hannibal, rank him as a commander, settle every disputed event in the Second Punic War, or reconstruct Carthaginian culture from hostile or incomplete surviving evidence.

```text
NAME != ENTITY
ENTITY != CLAIMS ABOUT ENTITY
REPRESENTATION_OF_ENTITY != DIRECT_EVIDENCE_OF_ENTITY
LATER_SOURCE != CONTEMPORARY_WITNESS
SURVIVING_RECORD != COMPLETE_RECORD
UNKNOWN != ABSENT
DEAD != UNPROTECTED
```

## Why this case was selected

The first three Human Record cases stress an artwork and its provenance, a repeated historical claim and its source ancestry, and a living craft's transmission lineage.

Hannibal introduces a different problem: **a particular deceased human whose present identity is reconstructed through a distributed and asymmetric historical record**.

The case should pressure-test whether THR can preserve:

- one historical entity across multiple names and later references without merging unrelated people named Hannibal;
- claims about that entity separately from the entity anchor;
- ancient literary sources separately from later retellings and representations;
- source dependence rather than counting repeated traditions as independent witnesses;
- uncertainty created by lost or unavailable Carthaginian-side material;
- later visual or cultural representations without treating them as evidence of Hannibal's appearance or exact actions;
- a deceased-subject boundary without pretending that death makes every inference or representation harmless or certain.

## Provisional entity anchor

Working entity: **Hannibal Barca**, the Carthaginian commander associated with the Second Punic War and the invasion of Italy beginning in 218 BCE.

This anchor is deliberately thin. It is a record-routing object, not a completed person profile.

Candidate identity structure:

```text
SOURCE-LITERAL MENTION
-> ZERO / ONE / MANY ENTITY CANDIDATES
-> RESOLVED / CANDIDATE / UNRESOLVED
-> CLAIMS REMAIN SEPARATE
```

`Hannibal`, `Hannibal Barca`, translated or transliterated forms, and descriptive phrases such as `the Carthaginian commander` may refer to the same historical candidate in a particular source context. The literal name alone is not sufficient identity proof.

## First bounded evidence path: the Alpine crossing

A strong starting assertion is intentionally modest:

> Ancient surviving literary sources report Hannibal leading a Carthaginian force across the Alps into Italy during the Second Punic War.

Polybius, *Histories* Book 3, gives an extended account of the campaign and Alpine crossing. Livy, *History of Rome* Book 21, gives another extended account and explicitly discusses uncertainty over the route through the Alps.

Public reading surfaces checked 17 September 2026:

- Polybius, *Histories*, Book 3, public-domain Loeb translation surface: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/3*.html
- Livy, *History of Rome*, Book 21, Perseus: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0144:book=21
- Livy, Book 21 chapter 38, Perseus, on disagreement about the Alpine route: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:abo:phi,0914,00121:38

The crossing itself and the exact route are different propositions.

```text
ALPINE_CROSSING_REPORTED != EXACT_PASS_ESTABLISHED
TWO_SURVIVING_AUTHORS != TWO_INDEPENDENT_EYEWITNESSES
ANCIENT_SOURCE != CONTEMPORARY_WITH_EVENT
```

A modern Livius comparison places Polybius and Livy side by side and argues that their similarities indicate shared source ancestry for at least parts of the crossing narrative. THR should therefore not count textual agreement mechanically as independent corroboration.

Discovery/secondary comparison surface checked 17 September 2026:
- Livius, *Hannibal in the Alps*: https://www.livius.org/sources/content/hannibal-in-the-alps/

This secondary analysis is useful for source-relationship discovery; it is not itself ancient evidence for the event.

## A claim with a different evidence shape: the childhood oath

Livy Book 21 reports a story that Hannibal, as a boy, was made by his father Hamilcar to swear hostility toward Rome. Livy Book 35 later places an account of the oath in a speech attributed to Hannibal at the court of Antiochus.

Public reading surfaces checked 17 September 2026:

- Livy Book 21: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0144:book=21
- Livy Book 35 chapter 19: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0165:book=35:chapter=19

This record does not yet establish the historical event behind the oath tradition. It records that the tradition exists in surviving Livian material and that different textual presentations must not be silently collapsed into direct Hannibal testimony.

```text
ANCIENT_AUTHOR_REPORTS_SPEECH != VERBATIM_SPEECH_SURVIVES
REPORTED_OATH_TRADITION != OATH_EVENT_INDEPENDENTLY_ESTABLISHED
FIRST_PERSON_WORDS_IN_LIVY != HANNIBAL-AUTHORED_TEXT
```

## The missing-side problem

Hannibal was Carthaginian. Much of the surviving narrative available to modern readers comes through Greek and Roman literary transmission rather than a surviving Hannibal-authored account of his life.

This record should not turn that asymmetry into a stronger claim than the evidence supports. The important THR structure is simply that **survival of evidence is itself aperture-bearing**.

```text
NO_SURVIVING_HANNIBAL_ACCOUNT_FOUND_HERE != HANNIBAL_WROTE_NOTHING
NO_SURVIVING_CARTHAGINIAN_SOURCE_FOUND_HERE != CARTHAGINIANS_RECORDED_NOTHING
SURVIVING_ROMAN_OR_GREEK_ACCOUNT != COMPLETE_CARTHAGINIAN_PERSPECTIVE
LOSS_OF_SOURCE != EVIDENCE_OF_NONEXISTENCE
```

A later pass should identify the strongest scholarly owners for Punic/Carthaginian textual survival and the source traditions used by Polybius and Livy before making a stronger statement about what was lost.

## Representation boundary

Later busts, paintings, films, illustrations, games and generated images can be records of **how Hannibal was represented later**. They are not automatically evidence of his historical appearance, words or actions.

```text
REPRESENTATION_OF_HANNIBAL != PORTRAIT_FROM_LIFE
POPULAR_IMAGE != HISTORICAL_APPEARANCE
DEPICTION_OF_EVENT != WITNESS_OF_EVENT
```

If THR later records a specific Hannibal representation, that representation should receive its own provenance chain and relationship to the historical-entity anchor.

## Deceased-subject boundary

Hannibal cannot answer this record, consent to inclusion, correct an inference, or refuse a representation. That changes the available correction route; it does not make the record omniscient or morally consequence-free.

Correction therefore has to travel through stronger evidence, source criticism, later scholarship and transparent revision of the record.

```text
SUBJECT_CANNOT_ANSWER != RECORD_CANNOT_BE_CHALLENGED
DEAD != CERTAIN
DEAD != FREE_FOR_ALL
LATER_SCHOLARSHIP != SUBJECT_TESTIMONY
```

## What this candidate can currently say

At this first bounded pass:

- a historical human identified in surviving sources as Hannibal can be anchored separately from the literal names used for him;
- surviving ancient literary sources including Polybius and Livy report his Alpine crossing during the Second Punic War;
- Livy himself preserves disagreement about the exact Alpine route, so `crossed the Alps` must not be silently upgraded to `used pass X`;
- the childhood oath is a distinct reported tradition and should not be represented as a surviving Hannibal-authored statement;
- source agreement may share ancestry and must not be counted as independent simply because two texts survive;
- later representations of Hannibal need their own provenance and cannot establish his historical appearance by themselves;
- the asymmetry of the surviving record is part of what THR should expose rather than smooth away.

## What remains unknown / not yet checked

This first pass does not establish:

- Hannibal's exact birth or death date;
- the exact Alpine pass;
- the historical truth or exact wording of the childhood oath;
- Hannibal's appearance;
- the complete source ancestry behind Polybius and Livy;
- which Carthaginian/Punic records about Hannibal once existed or exactly why particular material did not survive;
- the full archaeology, numismatics or epigraphy relevant to Hannibal;
- the strongest current scholarly consensus on disputed biographical details;
- a complete identity-disambiguation set for every historical person named Hannibal.

Those are research routes, not empty fields to fill by confidence.
