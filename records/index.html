Each entry is a walkable account of what we can check, where the evidence came from, what remains uncertain, and how later corrections changed the record.

This is the human reading surface. It does not replace the underlying Markdown, JSON, source links or repository history. A readable summary is not new evidence.

The records are intentionally different. One follows the provenance of a human-made artwork. The other follows the ancestry of a historical claim as it moves across the web. The common rule is simple: preserve the path back to the evidence and keep uncertainty visible.

Winslow Homer, Camp Fire (1880)

Current state: the Human Record matches The Metropolitan Museum of Art's published attribution and the checked image bytes from 14 September 2026.

What it shows: how a present-day provenance record can name its owner source, exact digital copy, independence limits and correction history without pretending to witness the 1880 creation event.

Still unknown: custody before 1927, independent proof of the creation event, and whether this approach will help with genuinely ambiguous contemporary human/synthetic work.

The “80% of German flak crews died” claim

Current state: unsupported in the sources checked. The true aggregate mortality rate remains unknown in this record.

What it shows: how one dramatic claim can appear to gain support as videos, articles and summaries repeat or mutate it even when the evidentiary ancestry remains thin.

Still unknown: the true mortality rate, whether an earlier source contains the figure, and whether the number mutated from a different casualty statistic.

Reading rule

What a record is — and is not

A Human Record entry can contain institutional records, web pages, witness reports, hashes, corrections and inference. Those are not all the same kind of evidence. Repetition is not corroboration, a hash is not truth, and a corrected record does not change the original event.

If stronger evidence appears, the record should change visibly while the earlier state remains recoverable.

Machine discovery: record catalogue JSON. Full source and history: public repository.

Human-readable views are derived from the underlying public records. SUMMARY != SOURCE · UNKNOWN != ABSENT · PROVENANCE != CERTIFICATION
