Each entry is a walkable account of what we can check, where the evidence came from, what remains uncertain, and how later corrections changed the record.

This is the human reading surface. It does not replace the underlying Markdown, JSON, source links or repository history. A readable summary is not new evidence.

The records are intentionally different. One follows an artwork's provenance, one follows the ancestry of a repeated historical claim, one follows public evidence about a living craft crossing an extinction boundary, and one follows how a famous historical person remains reachable through partial surviving accounts. The common rule is simple: preserve the path back to the evidence and keep uncertainty visible.

Artwork provenance · Specimen 1

Winslow Homer, Camp Fire (1880)

Current state: the Human Record matches The Metropolitan Museum of Art's published attribution and the checked image bytes from 14 September 2026.

What it shows: how a present-day provenance record can name its owner source, exact digital copy, independence limits and correction history without pretending to witness the 1880 creation event.

Still unknown: ownership or custody before Thomas B. Clarke, continuous physical custody and legal title across the reported 1880–1927 route, the exact 1899 transfer mechanics, independent proof of the creation event, and whether this approach will help with genuinely ambiguous contemporary human/synthetic work.

Claim provenance · Case 1

The “80% of German flak crews died” claim

Current state: unsupported in the sources checked. The true aggregate mortality rate remains unknown in this record.

What it shows: how one dramatic claim can appear to gain support as videos, articles and summaries repeat or mutate it even when the evidentiary ancestry remains thin.

Still unknown: the true mortality rate, whether an earlier source contains the figure, and whether the number mutated from a different casualty statistic.

Living-knowledge lineage · Case 2

Sieve and riddle making returns from extinction

Current state: Heritage Crafts reports the practice as critically endangered; the public revival lineage is only partly reconstructed.

What it shows: a reported last maker, an extinction classification, public Red List/media attention, renewed practice and new training — while the relationship between transmission, reconstruction and newly learned practice remains less visible than the revival story.

Still unknown: what was transmitted, what was reconstructed or newly learned, which parts of the older practice survive, and how durable the current learning/training routes are.

Historical-person source survival · Record 4

Hannibal: the person and the accounts that remain

Current state: a bounded Greek/Roman-mediated reading path is public; source reports remain attributed and the complete historical source genealogy is not claimed.

What it shows: a famous name can remain culturally reachable while the route back to the person consists of partial surviving accounts, later testimony and explicit inspection limits. The person is not the biography that survived.

Still unknown: much of the Punic/Carthaginian aperture, complete source dependence, manuscript-level verification, the exact Alpine pass and many biographical disputes outside this record.

Reading rule

What a record is — and is not

A Human Record entry can contain institutional records, web pages, witness reports, hashes, corrections and inference. Those are not all the same kind of evidence. Repetition is not corroboration, a hash is not truth, and a record of a living practice is not the practice itself.

If stronger evidence appears, the record should change visibly while the earlier state remains recoverable.
